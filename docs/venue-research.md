# Venue research

What the real venues look like, gathered to make the scenes recognisable. The venues in Deckhouse are fan-made recreations, not affiliated with or endorsed by the clubs or promoters. Photos were used only as reference: nothing here is traced or shipped, and the scenes carry no club logos or artworks.

**How this was researched (October 2026).** Web search worked from this environment, but the network proxy blocked fetching most pages directly (Wikipedia, Mixmag, Resident Advisor, Nomado and others). So the facts below come from search-result summaries of the cited pages, plus the reference photos supplied earlier in the project (the Circoloco booth, the Circoloco room and Boiler Room LA). The photos were not re-attached this time, so the scene notes written from them at the time are used instead. Anything not confirmed by a source is marked **unverified**. Video of recorded sets could not be watched from here.

## DC-10, Ibiza (Circoloco)

| | Finding | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Building | A converted finca (farmhouse) with whitewashed walls, at the end of Ibiza airport's runway. The name comes from the McDonnell Douglas DC-10 aircraft. | [xceed](https://xceed.me/ibiza/club/dc10-ibiza), [Nomado](https://www.nomadotravel.app/en/attractions/dc10-ibiza) |
| Rooms | Three: the Terrace, the Main Room and the open-air Garden. | [Nomado](https://www.nomadotravel.app/en/attractions/dc10-ibiza), [Crack](https://crackmagazine.net/article/news/15-years-of-circo-loco/) |
| Terrace | Famously open-air, with planes roaring low overhead; it has since been roofed. Planes are still seen passing overhead. | [xceed](https://xceed.me/ibiza/club/dc10-ibiza) |
| Main Room | A dark "sweatbox": so dark that coming in from the garden or terrace "all you see is black" for about a minute; warm and cosy; a deep bass felt in the chest. | [Crack](https://crackmagazine.net/article/news/15-years-of-circo-loco/2), [RA review](https://es.ra.co/reviews/21309) |
| Lighting | Red-drenched spotlights in keeping with Circoloco's red, black and white theme; a minimal lighting system compared with other Ibiza clubs. | [Tickets Ibiza](https://ticketsibiza.com/nl/clubs/dc10/) |
| Sound | Main room: a left/centre/right (L/C/R) system of custom Loud Professional cabinets (F215 tops, VH Sub121H/Sub118R subs, VH F15) on Powersoft X8 amps. Each area has its own DJ booth. | [Sound Light Up](https://en.soundlightup.com/news/powersoft-and-loud-professional-upgrade-for-ibizas-legendary-dc10-nightclub.html), [Fast and Wide](https://www.fast-and-wide.com/more/wideangle/11986-case-study-ibiza-s-dc10) |
| Booth and crowd | From the reference photos: a raised booth with cream booth monitors hanging over the DJ and the crowd pressed right up to it; orange globe lamps over the floor; warm bulb strings; red laser sheets; a fan wheel on the wall. | reference photos |
| Size and ceiling | Not found. The photos suggest a low ceiling (about 4–5 m). **Unverified.** | — |

**Palette.** Near-black room, deep red spot light, warm orange-amber practical lamps, white highlights.

### Gaps against the current scene, ranked
1. **"circoloco" lettering on the back wall.** It imitates the promoter's wordmark, so replace it with an unbranded element. (Compliance fix.)
2. **Not dark enough.** The ambient/hemisphere fill and haze make the room readable. The real room is near-black, lit by red spots and the warm practical lamps. Lower the fill, push the light into red pools and keep the globes and bulbs as the main glow.
3. **Speakers don't match the L/C/R system.** The hanging line arrays don't match. Use left/centre/right clusters facing the floor from the booth wall.
4. **Ceiling may be too high.** The scene has 5.3 m; the photos suggest lower and more cramped. Bring it down a little and keep the crowd tight.
5. **No sign of the finca.** Add a whitewashed doorway on one side opening onto a lighter terrace or garden. It tells you where you are and shows the darkness contrast the reviews describe.
6. **Planes.** These belong to the terrace, not the main room. Only visible through the doorway, if at all. (Low priority.)

## Boiler Room (the Los Angeles outdoor session)

| | Finding | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Format | The DJ faces away from (or across) a single camera, with friends dancing, smoking and chatting behind them; the audience is in frame, with no editing. | [Inspired by Beatz](https://www.inspiredbybeatz.com/en/boiler-room-15-years-club-culture/) |
| Lighting | "No fancy stage lights": a DJ booth surrounded by people. The focus is the people, not production. | [Techno Airlines](https://www.technoairlines.com/blog/what-is-a-boiler-room-all-you-need-to-know) |
| LA session | From the reference photo: an outdoor night session downtown; the crowd packed all around and behind the DJ with phones up; a red neon ring hanging on wires; a truss tower with a hot lamp; trees; a lit tower (City Hall) behind; the stream camera in front of the decks with a monitor. | reference photo |
| LA specifics | The exact location and date could not be confirmed by search. **Unverified.** | — |

**Palette.** Sodium/amber street light, red neon, cool LED from phones, warm hot-lamp key light on the DJ; an orange-brown LA sky glow with no stars.

### Gaps against the current scene, ranked
1. **"BOILER ROOM" text in the neon ring.** That's their branding; keep the plain red ring and remove the text, including from the venue-picker card. (Compliance fix.)
2. **Too much stage production.** Lasers and moving-head beams read as a festival. Keep the hot lamp and a few PARs, make the moving heads subtle, and drop the lasers from this venue.
3. **Sky and city.** Use a light-polluted LA sky (orange-brown glow, no stars), a skyline with more depth and variation, and warm streetlights that throw pools on the paving.
4. **Trees are flat, faceted blobs.** Give them more depth and back-lighting from the street lamps.
5. **Ground.** The plaza paving needs texture, kerbs and light pools.

## Berghain, Berlin (main floor)

| | Finding | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Building | The former Friedrichshain combined heat and power plant, built in 1953 and converted in 2003–04. The main floor opened in December 2004, Panorama Bar upstairs in October 2004. | [Thrillzing](https://thrillzing.com/music-venues/berghain-berlin/), [Berlin Tourism](https://berlintourism.org/berghain-berlin-guide/) |
| Main floor | Carved out of the turbine hall: 18 m concrete ceilings, exposed steel pillars, industrial fixtures and pipes still in place, almost no decoration. | [Berlin Tourism](https://berlintourism.org/berghain-berlin-guide/), [Nomado](https://www.nomadotravel.app/en/attractions/berghain) |
| Lighting | Minimal: lights appear sporadically, there is "a single fixed light bar running across the room", and there are no mirrors or reflective surfaces. | [Nomado](https://www.nomadotravel.app/en/attractions/berghain) |
| Booth | Recessed into the back wall; the artist is not the focal point. No stage, no VIP area, no bottle service. (Panorama Bar's booth, a table hung on chains behind a steel-tube railing, is upstairs.) | [Nomado](https://www.nomadotravel.app/en/attractions/berghain), [Slow Travel Berlin](https://www.slowtravelberlin.com/berghain-panorama-bar) |
| Layout | The main bar is in the south-west corner with a small seating annex; there is a darkroom area. | [Slow Travel Berlin](https://www.slowtravelberlin.com/berghain-panorama-bar) |
| Sound | Funktion-One; large stacks in each corner. The original "Dance Stack" (2005) was replaced in 2023. | [Berlin Tourism](https://www.berlintourism.org/?p=418), [DJ Mag](https://djmag.com/top100clubs/2025/16/berghain) |
| Capacity and policy | About 1,500. Phone cameras are stickered at the door (no photos). | [Thrillzing](https://thrillzing.com/music-venues/berghain-berlin/) |
| Balcony | The scene's steel balcony along one wall is **unverified** against the main floor. | — |

**Palette.** Grey concrete, black steel, cold white light, occasional red work lights, deep shadow.

### Gaps against the current scene, ranked
1. **The booth sits on a 9 m wide, 1 m high concrete stage.** The real booth is recessed into the back wall with no stage. Build a recessed booth niche and remove the stage riser.
2. **Lasers.** They're not part of the room's minimal look; remove them.
3. **No light bar.** Add a single fixed light bar across the room. Keep the beams sparse, the strobe bank and the red work lamps.
4. **Speaker stacks are in the wrong place.** Two are by the booth and two on the side walls; the stacks belong in the corners of the room.
5. **No bar.** Add a dim bar in one corner.
6. **Too much fill light.** Make the room darker, with more black between pools of light.

## Printworks, London (Press Halls)

| | Finding | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Status | Closed in 2023. Southwark Council approved redevelopment in September 2024, with the Press Halls kept as a venue and a reopening planned for 2026 as a mixed cultural venue (concerts, immersive shows, events and exhibitions). No report was found that it has actually reopened as of October 2026. | [BPM](https://bpm-mag.com/printworks-london-2026-council-approval), [Mixmag](https://mixmag.net/read/londons-printworks-will-reopen-2026-organisers-broadwick-live-news) |
| Building | The former Daily Mail / Evening Standard printing plant in Surrey Quays: about 10,000 m² in all. The plant was built inside a sound-insulated shell. | [ArchiExpo](https://emag.archiexpo.com/abandoned-newspaper-printing-plant-into-multi-purpose-cultural-venue/) |
| Press Halls | 16.8 m high and roughly 120 × 30 m (other sources say about 100 m long), named after the original printing presses that still flank its sides. Dark outlines on the floor trace where presses stood. Much of the original machinery is still lit. | [LSi](https://lsionline.com/news/digital-insanity-turns-to-green-hippo), [ArchiExpo](https://emag.archiexpo.com/abandoned-newspaper-printing-plant-into-multi-purpose-cultural-venue/) |
| Lighting | Kinesys motorised rigs (pods that move up and down); 17 LED strips down each side of the room, plus strips in the pods (over 180 m of LED strip in total); lasers on beams hung from the roof on chain hoists. | [LSi](https://lsionline.com/news/digital-insanity-turns-to-green-hippo), [PLSN](https://plsn.com/?p=275417) |
| Sound | Stacks of more than 40 d&b audiotechnik speakers arranged around the main stage. | [ArchiExpo](https://emag.archiexpo.com/abandoned-newspaper-printing-plant-into-multi-purpose-cultural-venue/) |
| Capacity | About 5,000–6,000 across the venue. | [DJ Mag](https://djmag.com/top100clubs/2018/14/Printworks-London) |

**Palette.** Black steel and concrete, cold white LED strips, warm sodium-like machinery light, white strobe hits.

### Gaps against the current scene, ranked
1. **The hall is too short.** It's 68 m long; the real one is 100–120 m. Lengthen it to about 110 m and run the gantries the full length.
2. **No presses along the sides.** Add dark, part-lit press machinery along both side walls at floor level, and press outlines on the floor.
3. **No LED strips down the sides.** Add rows of cold white LED strips along the walls that chase with the music. They're the room's signature in footage.
4. **The rig should read as moving pods.** It currently moves as one lowering grid; give it separate pods on chains (the drop behaviour stays the same).
5. **Speakers.** Use d&b-style stacks either side of the stage (low priority).

## Deckhouse Warehouse (fictional)

Based on large warehouse clubs such as Depot Mayfield (home of The Warehouse Project, Manchester): a 7,500-capacity main room in an old railway depot, where giant steel pillars each carry a pair of vertical LED batten bars, with hybrid strobes and a semi-transparent LED screen behind the stage. ([LSi](https://lsionline.com/news/glp-dominates-warehouse-project-main-rig))

### Gaps against the current scene, ranked
1. **No pillars.** Add steel pillars down both sides of the floor, each with a pair of vertical LED bars that pulse with the show.
2. **No room edges.** Add a bar along one side and green emergency-exit signs, so it reads as a real venue rather than a void.
3. **Surfaces.** Make the concrete floor and walls richer, with a slightly polished floor that picks up light.

## Across all venues
- Venues are darker and more contrasty than the scenes: light comes in pools from fixtures, not from ambient fill.
- Real rooms have edges: bars, exit signs, doors, pipes and ducts, cables and gaffer tape around the booth.
- People cast shadows and stand in shadow; the floor under a crowd is dark.
- No club logos or wordmarks.
