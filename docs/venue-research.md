# Venue research

What the real venues look like, gathered to make the scenes recognisable. The venues in Deckhouse are fan-made recreations, not affiliated with or endorsed by the clubs or promoters. Photos were used only as reference: nothing here is traced or shipped, and the scenes carry no club logos or artworks.

**How this was researched (October 2026).** Web search worked from this environment, but the network proxy blocked fetching most pages directly (Wikipedia, Mixmag, Resident Advisor, Nomado and others). So the facts below come from search-result summaries of the cited pages, plus the reference photos supplied earlier in the project (the Circoloco booth, the Circoloco room and Boiler Room LA). The photos were not re-attached this time, so the scene notes written from them at the time are used instead. Anything not confirmed by a source is marked **unverified**. Video of recorded sets could not be watched from here.

**Palette.** Near-black room, deep red spot light, warm orange-amber practical lamps, white highlights.

### Gaps against the current scene, ranked
1. **"circoloco" lettering on the back wall.** It imitates the promoter's wordmark, so replace it with an unbranded element. (Compliance fix.)
2. **Not dark enough.** The ambient/hemisphere fill and haze make the room readable. The real room is near-black, lit by red spots and the warm practical lamps. Lower the fill, push the light into red pools and keep the globes and bulbs as the main glow.
3. **Speakers don't match the L/C/R system.** The hanging line arrays don't match. Use left/centre/right clusters facing the floor from the booth wall.
4. **Ceiling may be too high.** The scene has 5.3 m; the photos suggest lower and more cramped. Bring it down a little and keep the crowd tight.
5. **No sign of the finca.** Add a whitewashed doorway on one side opening onto a lighter terrace or garden. It tells you where you are and shows the darkness contrast the reviews describe.
6. **Planes.** These belong to the terrace, not the main room. Only visible through the doorway, if at all. (Low priority.)

## Boiler Room (the Los Angeles outdoor session)

| | Finding | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Format | The DJ faces away from (or across) a single camera, with friends dancing, smoking and chatting behind them; the audience is in frame, with no editing. | [Inspired by Beatz](https://www.inspiredbybeatz.com/en/boiler-room-15-years-club-culture/) |
| Lighting | "No fancy stage lights": a DJ booth surrounded by people. The focus is the people, not production. | [Techno Airlines](https://www.technoairlines.com/blog/what-is-a-boiler-room-all-you-need-to-know) |
| LA session | From the reference photo: an outdoor night session downtown; the crowd packed all around and behind the DJ with phones up; a red neon ring hanging on wires; a truss tower with a hot lamp; trees; a lit tower (City Hall) behind; the stream camera in front of the decks with a monitor. | reference photo |
| LA specifics | The exact location and date could not be confirmed by search. **Unverified.** | — |

**Palette.** Sodium/amber street light, red neon, cool LED from phones, warm hot-lamp key light on the DJ; an orange-brown LA sky glow with no stars.

### Gaps against the current scene, ranked
1. **"BOILER ROOM" text in the neon ring.** That's their branding; keep the plain red ring and remove the text, including from the venue-picker card. (Compliance fix.)
2. **Too much stage production.** Lasers and moving-head beams read as a festival. Keep the hot lamp and a few PARs, make the moving heads subtle, and drop the lasers from this venue.
3. **Sky and city.** Use a light-polluted LA sky (orange-brown glow, no stars), a skyline with more depth and variation, and warm streetlights that throw pools on the paving.
4. **Trees are flat, faceted blobs.** Give them more depth and back-lighting from the street lamps.
5. **Ground.** The plaza paving needs texture, kerbs and light pools.

5. **No bar.** Add a dim bar in one corner.
6. **Too much fill light.** Make the room darker, with more black between pools of light.

### Gaps against the current scene, ranked
1. **The hall is too short.** It's 68 m long; the real one is 100–120 m. Lengthen it to about 110 m and run the gantries the full length.
2. **No presses along the sides.** Add dark, part-lit press machinery along both side walls at floor level, and press outlines on the floor.
3. **No LED strips down the sides.** Add rows of cold white LED strips along the walls that chase with the music. They're the room's signature in footage.
4. **The rig should read as moving pods.** It currently moves as one lowering grid; give it separate pods on chains (the drop behaviour stays the same).
5. **Speakers.** Use d&b-style stacks either side of the stage (low priority).

## Deckhouse Warehouse (fictional)

Based on large warehouse clubs such as Depot Mayfield (home of The Warehouse Project, Manchester): a 7,500-capacity main room in an old railway depot, where giant steel pillars each carry a pair of vertical LED batten bars, with hybrid strobes and a semi-transparent LED screen behind the stage. ([LSi](https://lsionline.com/news/glp-dominates-warehouse-project-main-rig))

### Gaps against the current scene, ranked
1. **No pillars.** Add steel pillars down both sides of the floor, each with a pair of vertical LED bars that pulse with the show.
2. **No room edges.** Add a bar along one side and green emergency-exit signs, so it reads as a real venue rather than a void.
3. **Surfaces.** Make the concrete floor and walls richer, with a slightly polished floor that picks up light.

**What the scene takes from this:**
- **Haze:** beams and lasers only show in haze, and thin out in clear air.
- **Hits:** lasers end where they land, with dots on the vault, walls and floor.
- **Colour:** RGB primaries, with white mixed from them.
- **Height:** every beam stays above head height. The liquid sky is a flat sheet 3 m above the floor, not a face-level scan.
- **Strobes:** a "Reduce flashing" setting caps them at 3 flashes a second.
- **IMAG:** a light broadcast grade, with cuts between cameras.

### What the scene does
- **The hall at its published size:**
  - 116.6 × 55.11 m
  - a segmental fabric vault springing at 14 m and peaking at 25 m, in ten bays between ribs on steel columns
  - doors and exits along the walls
  - the organ on its gallery with five pipe towers, the rose window behind it, and a bar under the gallery
- **Arena:**
  - a 23 × 7 m LED wall with the visual player, cropped to the wall's shape, and two portrait LED towers showing a live pit camera on the DJ (IMAG)
  - flown main, sub and side hangs with delays halfway down, a sub line in the pit, front fills and a barrier
  - three stage trusses and three audience trusses with 62 moving heads (8 of them standing upstage, firing up), 35 strobe bars and 8 blinders
  - lasers from the stage and above the wall, CO2 and cold sparks
  - a mix position with engineers at the desk, and bars
  - about 8,000 people: 516 jointed dancers at the front and 7,450 animated cut-outs behind them
- **In the round:**
  - a 1.5 m round riser with a light rim, a barrier ring and four sub stacks
  - 43,125 hanging lights over the floor, playing kick ripples, build-up sweeps, drop flashes and colour clouds
  - a surround PA of eight hangs, a few corner beams, and a mix position behind the DJ
  - about 8,400 people all the way round

## Across all venues
- Venues are darker and more contrasty than the scenes: light comes in pools from fixtures, not from ambient fill.
- Real rooms have edges: bars, exit signs, doors, pipes and ducts, cables and gaffer tape around the booth.
- People cast shadows and stand in shadow; the floor under a crowd is dark.
- No club logos or wordmarks.

## What phase 4 changed

Each ranked gap above, as of the end of the October 2026 overhaul. **Done** means it's in the scene; **partly** and **not done** say what's missing.

**DC-10**
1. Lettering: **done.** The back wall has an unbranded slat panel; the text is gone from the venue card too.
2. Darkness: **partly.** Fill is down by about a third, the ceiling is lower, and the venue keeps ACES tone mapping for deep blacks. The globes, bulb strings and red spots still light much of the room, so it reads as dark red rather than near-black.
3. L/C/R speakers: **done.** Left, centre and right clusters hang off the booth wall; the subs stay on the floor.
4. Ceiling: **done** (5.3 m to 4.8 m).
5. The finca: **done.** A whitewashed doorway in the left wall glows warm, with silhouettes beyond it.
6. Planes: **not done.** The terrace isn't modelled.

**Boiler Room LA**
1. Ring lettering: **done**, also removed from the venue card.
2. Production: **partly.** The lasers are gone; the moving heads are unchanged.
3. Sky and city: **partly.** A light-polluted sky glow with no stars; the skyline and street-light pools are unchanged.
4. Trees: **not done.**
5. Ground: **partly.** Matte paving with relief and joints; no kerbs or light pools.

**Berghain**
1. Recessed booth: **done.** No stage; the booth sits in a niche in the back wall behind a steel rail, with a dim strip under the lintel.
2. Lasers: **done** (removed).
3. Light bar: **done.** One fixed bar spans the room; the beams stay sparse, and the strobes and red work lamps are kept.
4. Stacks: **done.** One stack in each corner, turned to the floor.
5. Bar: **done.** A dim bar sits in a far corner (which corner, against the real room's south-west, isn't modelled), plus exit signs.
6. Fill: **partly.** The VIP guests and the stage are gone, and the room is bare concrete under ACES. The fill level is unchanged: a darker pass made the floor unreadable.
- The balcony is still **unverified** and was left in.

**Printworks**
1. Length: **done.** 112 m, with the gantries and columns the full length.
2. Presses: **done.** Dark, part-lit machinery along both sides, and press outlines on the floor.
3. LED strips: **done.** 17 cold strips up each side wall, chasing on the beat.
4. Pods: **not done.** The rig still lowers as one grid.
5. d&b-style stacks: **not done.**

**Deckhouse Warehouse**
1. Pillars: **done.** Steel pillars down both sides, each with two LED battens that chase down the room.
2. Edges: **done.** Block walls and a ceiling, a bar along the left wall, exit signs.
3. Surfaces: **done.** A polished floor with relief and worn patches; concrete-block walls.

**Across all venues:**
- per-venue grade and tone mapping
- real edges: bars, exits, DC-10's doorway, and booth clutter (laptop, drinks, cables, gaffer tape)
- contact shadows under every dancer
- no club logos or wordmarks anywhere, venue cards included
